Ireland's Life Sciences Sector Is Experiencing Unprecedented Growth

For over 20 years, LSC has proudly been at the forefront of the life sciences industry. We've been connecting pharmaceutical consultants with cutting edge contracts in Ireland and Denmark. Ireland's life sciences sector is experiencing unprecedented growth, with more than €5 billion in investments currently being poured into large scale pharma and MedTech projects across the country, signalling a powerful vote of confidence in Ireland's talent, infrastructure, and global standing as a life sciences leader. From state of the art biologics facilities to cutting edge device manufacturing hubs, these projects aren't just expanding capacity, they're reshaping the future of healthcare, driving regional development, and creating thousands of high value jobs. At LSC, we've mapped out the key projects that are shaping this next chapter, showing you where investment is happening, who's driving it, and what it means for the future of the industry in Ireland. Whether you're a professional seeking your next opportunity, or if you are just looking to stay informed, this gives you a clear snapshot of the momentum behind Ireland's life sciences success story.

A Nationwide Growth Story

Cork and Dublin hold the largest share of the projects we're tracking, including Cork's investments across Carrigtwohill, Blarney and Shanbally, and Dublin's high profile projects like Bristol Myers Squibb, Pfizer and West Pharmaceutical Services. But beyond these two hubs, there are just as many significant projects taking shape across the country. Limerick is now home to one of Eli Lilly's most significant global investments. Galway is building Dexcom's first manufacturing site anywhere in Europe. Kerry is hosting its biggest life sciences investment to date through Astellas. Offaly has quietly become a hub for veterinary biologics through Zoetis. Mayo continues to grow through Hollister's ongoing commitment to Ballina. Westmeath is now home to Novo Nordisk's newest European facility. And Louth has become the site of MSD's first fully owned vaccine manufacturing plant in Ireland. Taken together, this tells a clear story. Ireland's life sciences growth is not confined to its traditional hubs. If you've been holding back from applying because you assumed the opportunity was only in Dublin or Cork, that assumption no longer holds. Wherever you are based, there is very likely a well funded, actively growing project closer to you than you think.

Where Ireland's Growth Is Really Coming From

Look closely across these projects and a clear pattern starts to emerge. Eli Lilly's Kinsale facility, Novo Nordisk's new Athlone site, and West Pharma's Damastown expansion all exist because of the same global shift, the surge in diabetes and obesity treatment. Dexcom's continuous glucose monitoring investment sits right alongside it, part of the same wave of demand for better tools to manage metabolic health. This is not a coincidence. It is one of the fastest growing corners of the entire pharmaceutical industry today, and Ireland has positioned itself at the centre of manufacturing it for the world. From peptide production to next generation drug delivery systems, the country is becoming a genuine global hub for the medicines behind one of healthcare's biggest breakthroughs of this decade. Understanding where a company sits within this wave tells you something real about its momentum and its future, not just its current headcount. For candidates, that means these are not simply job openings. They are a chance to build a career at the centre of where the industry is actually heading right now.

The Projects Behind The Numbers

A closer look at what each investment means and who is behind it.

Bristol Myers Squibb
Cruiserath, Dublin 15

BMS is building its first European sterile drug product facility here, a $400m signal that Ireland now sits at the centre of its global biologics strategy, not just its supply chain. The investment expands manufacturing across oncology, immunology and haematology and will push Cruiserath's headcount past 1,000 as the facility completes this year, adding 350 new roles along the way.

Pfizer
Grange Castle, Dublin

€1.2bn, the largest single investment Pfizer has ever made in Ireland, is doubling biological drug substance capacity at a site that has already punched above its weight. Grange Castle helped manufacture Paxlovid and was one of the first Pfizer plants worldwide to produce mRNA drug substance for its Covid vaccine, a capability now feeding Pfizer's next generation pipeline, including a late stage flu vaccine candidate. Construction continues through 2027, creating 400 to 500 new roles and bringing Pfizer's Irish headcount to roughly 5,500.

Hollister Incorporated
Ballina, Co. Mayo

Nearly 50 years into its life in Mayo, Hollister isn't slowing down. €80m is going into R&D and digital transformation, with the ambition of making Ballina a global centre of expertise in device design. The investment creates approximately 50 new roles across engineering, data science and business services, at a site that already employs almost 1,000 people.

Zoetis
Tullamore, Co. Offaly

The world's largest animal health company has completed one of the most significant biologics expansions in the Midlands. The Phase 2 facility, worth close to €150m, is now moving through commissioning toward full operation this year, roughly quadrupling manufacturing capacity for veterinary monoclonal antibodies including Cytopoint, Librela and Solensia. The Tullamore team has already grown from 90 to 180 plus people since Zoetis acquired the site from Nexvet in 2017, with further roles expected as the facility ramps to full output.

Eli Lilly, Raheen
Limerick

This is the most ambitious manufacturing expansion Lilly has undertaken anywhere in its history. €927m is producing the biologic active ingredients behind its Alzheimer's disease portfolio, with sustainability built in from the design stage, including 35% lower energy intensity, 40% less water use and 15% less waste than standard biopharma manufacturing. The facility brings total site employment to 450, an additional 150 roles, as production ramps up this year.

Eli Lilly, Kinsale
Cork

An award winning, digital first continuous manufacturing platform. €726m and an ISPE Facility of the Year win for innovation, producing the complex peptides behind two of the fastest growing drug categories in the world, diabetes and obesity treatment, including Mounjaro and Zepbound. The facility has been producing medicines since 2023 and runs in part on Ireland's largest privately owned solar farm, a 26 acre installation unique among Irish pharma manufacturing sites.

Dexcom
Athenry, Co. Galway

Dexcom chose the west of Ireland for its first manufacturing site anywhere in Europe, a €300m, five year investment producing the continuous glucose monitors reshaping how people manage diabetes day to day. Ireland's talent pool and established medtech sector were the deciding factors over other European locations. The project creates up to 1,000 high skilled graduate and technician roles, alongside 500 construction jobs, with production ramping up this year.

Astellas
Tralee, Co. Kerry

€330m at Kerry Technology Park marks Kerry's biggest life sciences investment to date. The build has already created 600 construction jobs, and as the site comes online in phases through 2027 and 2028, it will add 100 permanent, highly specialised roles across engineering, science and technology.

Merck (Merck KGaA)
Blarney, Cork

This is Merck's first fully climate neutral manufacturing facility anywhere in its global network, and it's in Cork. €150m, part of a wider €440m commitment across Blarney and Carrigtwohill, produces the filtration technology used to manufacture vaccines, monoclonal antibodies and next generation cell and gene therapies. It opened in September 2025, with 200 plus roles expected by 2028.

GE Healthcare
Carrigtwohill, Cork

Three decades of manufacturing trust in Cork have earned GE Healthcare's confidence for a €132m expansion that will double annual output of contrast media, the imaging agents that make CT and X-ray diagnostics possible for patients worldwide. Construction began in February 2025 and targets completion by late 2027, with 250 construction phase roles created so far.

BioMarin
Shanbally, Cork

BioMarin's only manufacturing site outside the United States has just added a new four storey laboratory. €60m supports the company's mission of treating rare genetic conditions, on a site that already runs the complete production journey from bulk drug substance to finished, packaged medicine. This follows an earlier €38m expansion completed in 2023.

Stryker
Cork (Carrigtwohill and Anngrove)

Anngrove is Stryker's global centre of excellence for 3D printed orthopaedic implants, one of the most technically advanced additive manufacturing sites in the company's entire network. €200m in R&D investment across three Cork facilities, plus a purpose built additive manufacturing plant with capacity for 600 high tech jobs, has made Cork central to how Stryker builds the future of joint replacement technology. Stryker now employs over 4,000 people across its Cork sites combined.

MSD (Merck and Co.)
Dundalk, Co. Louth

A €500m move that made headlines across the pharma industry. Rather than continuing to contract manufacture here, MSD has taken full ownership of the site, its first fully owned live virus vaccine facility in Ireland. The 15,520 square metre, three storey operation covers drug substance, drug product and quality control. It currently employs around 200 people, with 150 further roles planned at Dundalk specifically, as part of MSD's wider commitment to add roughly 1,000 jobs across its eight Irish sites in Carlow, Cork, Dublin, Louth, Meath and Tipperary.

Novo Nordisk
Monksland, Athlone

The company behind Wegovy is putting €432m into the Irish Midlands, proof that this wave of investment isn't confined to Dublin and Cork. The new tabletting facility, announced earlier this year, will expand global manufacturing capacity for oral GLP-1 treatments, including oral Wegovy. The current site employs 260 people, and the broader 45 acre development is expected to generate up to 500 construction jobs through 2027 and 2028.

AbbVie
Carrigtwohill, Cork

€60m and a new generation of sterile manufacturing technology have brought 70 new roles to a Cork site that marked its 20th anniversary in 2022, quietly becoming a key production hub for AbbVie's aesthetics business, including Botox related manufacturing. Roles span sterile manufacturing, quality control and engineering, with manufacturing operations now underway since 2025.

West Pharmaceutical Services
Damastown, Dublin 15

330 new roles and a brand new 165,000 square foot facility, built for one purpose, keeping pace with surging global demand for injectable GLP-1 treatments. It represents a 30% jump in West's entire Irish workforce in a single expansion, bringing total headcount to 1,400 plus across Dublin and Waterford. It opened in March 2026, making it one of the most recent investments on this list.
